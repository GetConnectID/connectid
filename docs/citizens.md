# ConnectID Citizens

Every ConnectID user becomes a Citizen.

A Citizen is a participant in the ConnectID digital society, not simply an account or username.

## Becoming a Citizen

A person should be able to create a ConnectID identity without owning cryptocurrency.

The initial process should remain simple:

1. Create a ConnectID account
2. Choose a unique @username
3. Choose a display name
4. Choose or upload an avatar
5. Become a Citizen

A wallet is optional.

Users who do not own, use or trade cryptocurrency must be able to use ConnectID normally.

## Citizen Identity

Each Citizen has a unique ConnectID identity.

The public identity consists of:

- @username
- Display name
- Avatar
- Citizen status
- Reputation
- Connections
- Optional wallet connection

The ConnectID identity should remain independent from any cryptocurrency wallet.

## Wallet Connection

A Citizen may optionally connect one wallet.

The wallet can provide an additional verifiable connection between the Citizen and an external blockchain identity.

A wallet is not required to become a Citizen.

A wallet does not automatically determine reputation.

## Connections

ConnectID uses a connection-based social model.

A Citizen can connect with another Citizen.

Example:

Sean → Connect → John

After the connection is accepted, Citizens may eventually:

- Send messages
- Share content
- Invite each other to communities
- Participate in group conversations
- Use future ConnectID social features

ConnectID is therefore designed around connections rather than followers.

## Reputation

Every Citizen can build reputation through meaningful participation and contribution.

Reputation should be visible but should not discourage new or lower-ranked Citizens.

Reputation is earned through behaviour and contribution, not simply through owning cryptocurrency.

## Trust and Abuse Prevention

ConnectID should be designed to reduce fake accounts, bots, scams and abuse.

The system may use different signals to establish trust, including:

- Account history
- Participation
- Community behaviour
- Reputation
- Optional wallet verification
- Future verification mechanisms

These signals should support trust without making cryptocurrency ownership a requirement.

## Privacy

A Citizen may use a virtual identity.

A legal name is not required to be publicly displayed.

ConnectID should separate public identity from private information wherever possible.

## Future Development

The initial Citizen system should remain simple.

The architecture should allow future functionality such as:

- Multiple identity roles
- Advanced verification
- Expanded reputation systems
- Additional wallet functionality
- Communities
- Messaging
- Voice and video communication
- Other digital society features

The initial implementation should not require these future systems to exist before ConnectID can be released.

## Principle

Every Citizen should have a recognizable and accountable presence within ConnectID.

Citizens should be able to communicate and participate without cryptocurrency, while optional blockchain connections can provide additional verification and transparency.

ConnectID should remain accessible to everyone.
